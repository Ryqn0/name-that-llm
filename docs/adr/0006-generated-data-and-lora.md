# ADR 0006: Generated passages for training, and LoRA on 4-bit weights in our own loop

- **Status:** accepted (2026-10-27, P7). The pool policy is Claude's call, delegated by the
  user ("as you wish"). P8 amended it (2026-10-30): a pinned copy, and a rule for dropped
  sources.
- **Context:**
  - Our training data is small: 2,699 passages, 150 prompts answered by 18 models. The best
    ensemble scored 0.410 on the public leaderboard (P5), when the top 10% needed about 0.64.
  - Other participants published pools of passages generated by the same 18 models, as
    Kaggle datasets. The largest, `crtproduction/name-that-llm-open-pool`, gathers dozens of
    sources under CC BY-SA 4.0.
  - The project's design allowed generated data "only after the host's ruling". No ruling
    came.
  - The strongest member in reach was a fine-tuned LLM. An 8-billion-parameter model takes
    16 GB in float16, more than a T4 holds, and the T4 has no bfloat16.
- **Decision:**
  - **We train on the public pool, with three safeguards:**
    1. only free, public Kaggle datasets with an explicit open licence on their page (CC0,
       CC BY, CC BY-SA, Apache 2.0, MIT; "unknown" or "other" is refused). We never pay to
       generate data;
    2. the pool only ever trains. Every score is measured on our own passages, held out by
       prompt, after removing pool passages that copy them or answer a held-out prompt;
    3. one of the two final submissions stays pool-free (`ensemble`, LB 0.410), so a late
       ruling against generated data can't sink us.
  - **`lr` keeps an allowlist** of the sources that helped it when added alone: 5, then 3
    after the maintainer's clean release removed two (I-028, I-032).
  - **P8's amendments** (I-034, the user's call on drops):
    - every run reads our private, pinned copy of a release (`ryqn00/name-that-llm-pool-v10`);
    - a weekly check compares the latest release with the pin, source by source;
    - new runs leave out any source a later release dropped. A member already trained on it
      is retrained only on proof that the source breaks its licence.
  - **The LLM: LoRA on 4-bit weights**, with peft and bitsandbytes, inside our own training
    loop (ADR 0003), not Hugging Face's `Trainer`:
    - Qwen3-8B base with an 18-way classification head, all 36 layers;
    - rank 16, alpha 32, on every attention and MLP projection; 2 epochs at lr 1e-4;
      512 tokens; 5,000 pool passages per fold;
    - two folds at once, one per T4; at inference, the five fold adapters are averaged on
      one shared base.
- **Consequences:**
  - **The gains held on the hidden prompts.** `lr` with the pool went from CV 0.3212 to
    0.4338. The ensemble with it went from LB 0.410 to 0.454 (+0.044, against +0.049 in CV).
    The LLM alone scores 0.4705 on five folds, our best single member, and four members in
    a log blend reach 0.5246 (seed 42, nested weights).
  - **The cost is GPU time.** A pair of pooled folds takes about 4 hours on Kaggle's two
    T4s, so one seed of five folds took three sessions. There is one seed only, so the
    blend's weights come from nested cross-validation (P8 Task 3).
  - **A public dependency changes without notice.** The pool lost 2 of our sources on
    2026-10-29. On 2026-11-01 it dropped one more source, shrank eight and brought one back.
    Hence the pin and the weekly check. The submission reads `lr`'s pool from the pin, and
    stops if it isn't the measured 28,048 passages.
  - **The offline submission needs peft and bitsandbytes,** which the Kaggle image lacks.
    Their wheels ship in our lib dataset (`release-lib`).
  - **The adapters live in a private dataset,** 178 MB per fold. The notebook finds them by
    their folders (`*/llm/fold<k>/`), so a dataset's layout is part of its contract: the
    first upload had it wrong.
